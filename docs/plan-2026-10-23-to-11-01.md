# The October sprint — quality to 10/10 by 1 November

Written by the CEO, 2026-10-23. This is the plan the crew works to until the November build
starts. `docs/decisions/home-global-feed-vision.md` sets the sequence: **now → 31 Oct is quality
only**; the global home feed starts 1 November, not before.

## The target

By **1 November**, on the live product:

1. Every reader-facing surface passes the 10/10 gates in `docs/reader-quality-10-of-10.md` —
   delivery, significance, surfaces, alerts — measured by their own commands, exit 0.
2. The reader-clarity audit's **blockers are zero** and its should-fixes are closed or ruled.
3. A stranger can read home, the briefing and a story page the way they read Wikipedia: no
   unexpanded jargon, no paragraph in an order the reader has to rebuild, no chrome that claims
   something the product did not do.
4. The source pool is growing in the background — 205 → 300 validated feeds by 5 November — so
   home's promised depth exists when the November build needs it.

Quality is the target. Speed is the method, not the excuse: a merged fix that a reader still
cannot understand is not progress.

Both engineers are staffed at all times on disjoint files. Idle is a planning failure, not a
rest state.

## Rules the crew works under (restated, not implied)

1. **Workers push branches and open PRs. They never merge and never push `main`.** The CEO merges
   green PRs at the gate.
2. **Test-first.** The failing test is written against the audited, quoted string before the fix;
   a rule that only fires on the audit's own fixture is not a fix.
3. **Name an unmet rule as a finding.** Never tune an audit or a gate to make code pass. The 10/10
   bar is not negotiable by a worker or by the CEO.
4. **One card per 2–3 sections of a spec**, so a run cannot die of iteration budget. A crashed
   card gets a fresh, smaller successor, never an unchanged unblock.
5. **Disjoint files.** Each card names the other engineer's in-flight files as forbidden. Shared
   `docs/context.md` merges by union (`.gitattributes`), so overlap there is never a veto.
6. **Every claim carries its measurement** — the command, the number, the date. "Done" without a
   number is not done.
7. **The board is the queue.** At least one backlog card queued per engineer at all times; the
   staffing cron unblocks it the moment a slot frees.
8. **The user sees the work in Telegram** — short, concrete `forge:` / `rook:` / `scout:` comments
   on the card at each milestone, no narration.
9. **`nvm use 22`** for every nyuz command; Node 20 fails on native WebSocket.
10. **The live product is the judge.** A fix is accepted on the deployed surface a reader
    actually opens, not in a test file alone.

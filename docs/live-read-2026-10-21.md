## How this was read, and the one thing it could not be

A fresh browser with no cookies gets the marketing page at `/` and **a sign-in wall on every reader
route**: `/dashboard`, `/dashboard/briefing`, `/dashboard/history`, `/dashboard/alerts` and
`/dashboard/story/…` all answer HTTP 200 and then replace themselves with
`/auth?next=…` in the client (`00-stranger-landing.png`, `01-stranger-signin-wall.png`, both captured
in a clean context). The reader surfaces are authenticated; there is no anonymous briefing to read.

So the read below was taken in a browser that holds a reader session, **read-only**: navigated and
read, never clicked anything that writes — no Save, no vote, no install, no Email. The report is
written as a reader who knows nothing about the pipeline. Where a sentence only makes sense if you
know how it was produced, that is called a defect, not an explanation.

**Not measured:** mobile, email delivery, push, Telegram, and the `Email` button. Email is dark by
stage policy, so its absence is not a finding here.

**One caveat that matters for the error pages.** 6 of the 25 story pages failed on first open during
a back-to-back sweep of the deck. Each of those six, reopened one at a time with a pause, returned
200 and rendered fully. The failures are load-shaped, not page-shaped — but they were still what the
reader saw, so they are recorded as they happened and marked as retested.

Screenshots were captured locally and are not committed.

## 7. What holds up

- **24 of 25 card lines are the story page's own opening sentence.** Whatever else is wrong with the
  lines, the feed and the page do not tell the reader two different things — with the single
  exception of card 18.
- **No deal, coupon or first-party celebration card in the 25.** Nothing priced, nothing discounted,
  no vendor announcing its own award. The three refusal classes wired into the feed gate are not
  leaking into this deck.
- **No template failures.** `undefined`, `NaN` and `[object Object]` do not appear on the feed, the
  briefing, the archive, the alerts or the story page.
- **Every story page keeps its source attached**, with a working external link and `[1]` markers that
  resolve to it.
- **The archive rows open the day they name**, and the deck they open is the one that was delivered.
- **The alert channel is free of duplicates** in the eleven rows visible.

## 8. What I would fix first, in this order

1. **D1** — a sentence from another story is a claim the page cannot support. This outranks
   everything else here.
2. **D9** — the briefing page cannot be read from. It is the surface the feed's main button opens.
3. **D2, D3, D4, D7** — the card line is the first sentence a reader meets, and four of today's
   twenty-five are assembled wrong.
4. **D6** — the deck's premise is one event, once.
5. **D10** — six of twenty-five story opens returned an error page under ordinary back-to-back
   reading.
6. **D12, D13** — the alert channel's contract is silence unless it matters.
7. **D5, D11, D14, D15** — thin lines, deck size, archive copy, and a scoring block no reader asked
   for.
